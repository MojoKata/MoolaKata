# Adjusted options

When an underlying splits by a ratio that isn't a whole number, merges, spins off a company or pays
a large special dividend, the OCC adjusts its open options and says how in an information memo
(linked here). Type each open contract's new terms from it. The lots carry over to the adjusted
contract, with their days held and their basis: nothing is sold, and there is no wash sale.

A whole-number split (2:1, 4:1) needs none of this: recorded or imported on the stock, it takes the
account's open options with it, contracts times the ratio and strike divided by it. A contract
already adjusted to deliver cash or another company's shares is the exception: those don't split,
so it waits for the OCC's memo and is typed here.

It lists the options open in accounts you can change; with none, there is nothing to adjust.

## Underlying {#adjust-underlying}

The stock whose options were adjusted.

## Effective {#adjust-date}

The day the adjustment took effect.

## New Root {#new-root}

The adjusted contract's root, such as _XYZ1_. It is listed under it in the underlying's chain, and
its mark comes from there.

## Deliverable

What one contract delivers when assigned or exercised: shares other than 100 (_150 XYZ_ for a 3:2
split), shares of two companies (_100 XYZ + 20 ABC_), and any cash per contract. The strike and the
premium are spread over its parts by their value on the day.
