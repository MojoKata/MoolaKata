# Transaction

A change that moves the realized gains of a year whose return is filed asks first, naming the
year: **Change it anyway** makes it, and the return may need amending. The tax book lists the change
in its Issues until an amended return is added or someone says none is needed. While an amended
federal return is being prepared, the year is open and nothing is asked.

## Account

The account it belongs to.

## Date

As the institution wrote it.

## Kind

What it is: money in or out, a transfer, interest, a fee; or for an investing account a buy, a sale,
a dividend, shares moved in or out, coins received as income (staking, rewards: their quantity and
the day's price, no cash), or foreign tax a fund paid.

## Symbol

The instrument traded, such as `VTI`.

## Quantity

How many shares or units.

## Price

Per share or unit.

## Amount

The cash effect on the account: − for money out (a buy), + for money in (a sale), after fees.

## Fees

Commission and fees, 0 for none: a trade starts at 0, and left blank they're refused.

## Lots

A sale lists the lots held on its date: when each was bought, how many are held, their cost, and
whether a sale that day is long or short term. Type how many to take from each to pick them
(specific identification): the sale takes those, and what isn't picked goes oldest first. Leave
them empty and the account's lot method chooses. A lot a wash sale split is shown whole, and a pick
takes from both parts.

## Take {#take}

How many shares of that lot the sale takes; no more than the lot holds.

## Cost basis

For shares transferred in: what they cost. Without it the lot is marked **basis missing**.


## Lots, as bought {#transfer-lots}

For shares moved in from another broker: each lot as it was bought, its day, how many and what it
cost, adding up to the shares moved in, so a sale's term and gain are counted from the day bought.
None typed, they arrive as one lot whose day bought is missing: its term shows as unknown, and the
tax book lists it until you add it. Shares moved between two of your own accounts are offered as a
pair instead, and carry their lots with them.
## Payee

Who was paid, or who paid you.

## Category

Where the money went, for budgets and taxes. **Make a rule** files this payee the same way from
now on, from what you import.

## New category {#new-category}

What **New category…**, at the end of the list, adds: its name, up to 60 characters. It's made
when you save, and everyone sees it: the household has one tree of categories.

## Under {#new-category-under}

The top-level category it goes under, or none for a new top-level one. Categories have two
levels.

## Kind {#new-category-kind}

Expense, income, savings or transfer: how budgets count what's filed under it. Transfers never
count.

## Tax tag {#new-category-tax-tag}

Puts what's filed under it in Taxes › Deductions: charitable gifts, medical and dental, and the
others. Most categories have none.

## Split {#split}

One transaction across several categories; the parts must add up to its amount.

## Contribution year

For a retirement account: the tax year a contribution counts for. One made before the April deadline
may be for the year before.

## Contributed by {#contribution-source}

You, your employer (a match), a rollover, or a Roth conversion. A transfer matched from another
retirement account says it by itself.

## Deductible {#deductible}

For your own contribution to a traditional IRA: whether you deduct it on your return. **Not
deductible** contributions make your Form 8606 basis, so a later Roth conversion is taxed only on
the rest (the tax book's Retirement panel works it out). Until you choose, it is **Not decided**,
and a conversion that year waits for it. Deducted only in part? Split it: each part is marked.
Whether you can deduct it depends on your income and a workplace plan; your preparer decides.

## Memo

A note.

## Tags

Your own, separated by commas; others who share the account don't see them.

## Reimbursable

Spending someone pays back: an employer's expense, a roommate's half. It keeps its category, for taxes
and reports, and budgets don't count it while it's awaited. It's listed under **Reimbursements** on
Budgets, where a deposit that may pay it back is offered to pair; the paired part of the deposit
isn't income.

## Reimbursable amount

All of it unless you type less. Lower it to what was paid back, or turn **Reimbursable** off, and the
rest counts as spending again, in the month it was spent.

## Owed by

Who pays it back, as you want to see it: _Acme Corp_, _Sam_.

## Leave out {#leave-out}

Each budget counting this transaction, and what it counts as there. **Leave out** is that budget's
own, for a one-off such as a car bought outright: it then counts nowhere in that budget, and
Activity records it. The category is the transaction's, the same in every budget.

## Receipts

The receipts attached to it; each opens its file. **Add a receipt** uploads one or more and attaches
them here when you save. Whoever can read this account can see its receipts.

## Add a receipt {#add-receipt}

Upload PDFs or photos; they are kept at once, in the inbox, and attached to this transaction when
you save. ✕ leaves one in the inbox.
