# An Item

A physical asset: what it is, when and how it was acquired, its cost basis, and how it's valued.

Without a valuation, an item counts at its cost basis, depreciated when it depreciates.

## Name

What the item is called in the list: *American Gold Eagle, 1 oz*, *Rolex Submariner 124060*,
*Home*, *2023 Subaru Outback*.

## Address

Optional: a property's address, if you want it kept.

## Primary residence

Your main home. Its sale may be partly tax-free, which Taxes shows.

## Metal

Gold, silver, platinum or palladium: the spot price that values it.

## Form {#category}

A coin, bar, round or jewellery. A numismatic coin worth more than its metal belongs in a
collectables account instead.

## Category

The kind of collectable: a watch, art, wine, cards and so on.

## Valued by {#valuation}

**Valuations**: the latest dated value counts. **Depreciation**: a yearly percentage off the cost
basis, until a valuation replaces it.

## Weight per unit {#unit-weight}

The weight of one coin or bar, as stamped on it.

## Unit {#weight-unit}

Troy ounces, grams or kilograms. A troy ounce is 31.1 grams.

## Purity

The share of the weight that is the metal, as a fraction or in karats: .9999 for most gold coins and bars, .999 for silver, .925
for sterling, 22k (.9167) for a Krugerrand. Fine weight is weight × purity × quantity.

## Quantity

How many of them. Fine weight counts them all.

## Depreciation per year {#depreciation-rate}

A percentage of the value lost each year; about 12% suits most cars. A valuation replaces it.

## Date acquired {#acquired-on}

The day it was bought, given or inherited. A sale's holding period starts here.

## How acquired {#acquired-how}

Bought, a gift or inherited. A gift's basis is usually the giver's; an inherited item's is its value
at death.

## Cost basis {#cost-basis}

What it cost: the price plus premium, fees and shipping, or for a property the purchase price. A
gift's carried-over basis, or an inherited item's value at death. For a property, its improvements
add to it (Improvements, beside the fields).

## Closing costs

A property's costs at purchase that add to its basis.

## Insured value

What it's insured for, to compare with what it's worth.

## Selling premium {#sell-premium}

What you'd expect over spot when selling, in percent; under spot is a negative. 0% by default.

## Where it's kept {#location}

A safe, a bank box, a vault.

## Serial or certificate {#serial}

A serial, a grading certificate or a vehicle's VIN.

## Condition or grade {#condition}

Its condition, or its grade (MS-65, PCGS 64).

## Insurance policy {#policy}

The policy that covers it.

## Used personally

A car, a home, a watch you wear. A loss when it's sold isn't deductible.

## Notes

Anything else, in Markdown: provenance, service history.

## Valuations

Dated values with their source: an appraisal, a recent sale, a price guide or your estimate. The
latest counts; one over a year old is marked.

## Improvements

A property's improvements: a new roof, a remodel. Each adds to the cost basis on its date, with its
receipts kept as documents.

## Photos and Documents

The purchase receipt, an appraisal, a certificate, the title, photos. Kept as receipts, encrypted on
this server.

## Sale

The day it was sold, the proceeds and the fees, recorded when you save the item; **Undo the Sale**
takes it back, also when you save. The gain or loss is realized and shown in Taxes. A sale of
metals or collectables is marked **collectable**: taxed as a collectable, flagged on Form 8949. One
of property you used yourself is marked **personal**: a loss on it isn't deductible.
