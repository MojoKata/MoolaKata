# Projections

How your money might grow toward a goal, and whether it will pay for it: **retirement**, or a
**savings target** by a date. A model, not a forecast. (Education goals come with 529 accounts.)

![Projections](shots/projections.png)

A projection asks how your money will grow and what it will pay for: retirement, education, or a
savings target by a date.

## Active

The plans you follow. At most one per subject: one retirement plan for the same people, one per
savings target. Making one active stores its median path and its 10th–90th percentile band as of
that day: its **baseline**.

## Scenarios

Projections that aren't active: what-ifs to compare, such as retiring at 60 against 65, or saving
$500 more a month. **New projection** starts one.

## Tracking

For an active projection, the actual balance of what it is built on against its baseline: **on
track** inside the band, **ahead** above it, **behind** below it. Falling behind raises an alert,
checked at each month's end. In a projection shared with you, you see the status, and the balance
of only the accounts that are shared with you too; when some aren't, there is no band to show it
against.

## Show on my Dashboard {#show-on-dashboard}

For an active projection someone shares with you: show it on your Dashboard's Plans card. Off by
default.

## Dollars

**Nominal** dollars as they will be; **today's dollars** take inflation out.

## Built on

A portfolio, an account, or a typed amount for a what-if. Built on accounts, it starts from their
value today and their mix of asset classes; each class follows your assumptions in Profile.

## Left out {#left-out}

What a projection doesn't grow, named under its chart with its value today: a loan, a home, a
car or another item, and options. A projection grows cash, securities and precious metals, the
money that can be drawn on; a home isn't sold to pay for retirement, and a car loses value.

## Amount {#typed-amount}

The typed amount it starts from.

## Retirement age

When you retire: contributions stop, and withdrawals start, when the last of the people in it
retires.

## Partner retirement age

Your partner's, for a couple.

## Contribution per year {#contribution}

What is added each year until retirement.

## Grows yearly by (%) {#contribution-growth}

How much the contribution rises each year, with your pay.

## Withdrawal

How much is taken each year in retirement: the **4% rule** (4% of the balance at retirement, then
rising with inflation), a **fixed amount** rising with inflation, or a **percentage** of each year's
balance.

## Withdrawal a year, in today's dollars {#withdrawal-amount}

The fixed amount, before inflation.

## Percentage of the balance {#withdrawal-percent}

Taken from each year's balance.

## Other income {#other-income}

Social Security or a pension, from an age, a month. It covers part of each withdrawal.

## For {#beneficiary}

An education goal is for one person, one of the household's people. Their year of birth gives the
first year of school.

## Age school starts {#start-age}

18 for college; younger for a private school. School starts on Sep 1 of that year, and saving
counts until then.

## Years of school {#school-years}

How many years it pays for: four for a bachelor's degree.

## Cost per year {#cost-basis}

Tuition, fees, room and board for a year, in today's dollars: a shipped average for public four-year
(in-state) or private nonprofit four-year colleges, from the National Center for Education
Statistics, or a figure you type. It rises each year by education inflation, from your assumptions.

## Cost per year, typed {#cost}

Your own figure for a year, in today's dollars.

## Target amount {#target}

What the savings target is.

## Target date

By when.

## Saving per month {#monthly}

What goes in each month until then. The answer says what a month would reach it with a 90% chance,
or that no monthly saving reaches it by then.

## Fees a year (%) {#fees}

Fund and advisory fees, taken from the balance each year.

## Bitcoin model

For the bitcoin in what it is built on: your crypto assumption, or a model others publish. A model
with bear, base and bull cases shows those three paths instead of a fan.

## Run

Saves the inputs and works it out again. For an active projection it asks whether to move the
baseline to the new inputs.
