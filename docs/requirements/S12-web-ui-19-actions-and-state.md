# S12.19. Actions and the page's state

19. **Actions and the page's state.** What a person can't do is shown or hidden by why they
    can't, and what the page knows is shown briefly, its explanation in the guide (S12.7.4):
    1. **An action not open to them now**, by why:
       1. **Permission on this record** (it is shared with them to read; only its owner shares,
          deletes or changes its settings; another user's category) or **state they could
          change** (changes are to be saved first; a filing is needed first; the receipt is
          being read): the action stays where it is, unavailable. It looks off, reaches the
          keyboard, does nothing when pressed, and its tooltip says why (*Shared with you to
          read*, *Only its owner shares it*, *Save changes first*). It is never replaced by a
          line saying why.
       2. **Their role** (what only an admin does: Settings, Users and Groups): hidden. It is
          another level, never theirs to do.
       3. **A preference turned off** (a budget's moves): hidden, with no marker: they chose
          not to see it.
       4. What a person can only read is shown read (S12.18.6), never as a form of fields that
          are off: a dialog, a form, a contribution's source, a wallet's keys, a projection's
          inputs. A list of tasks only a record's writer can do (the bank transactions offered
          to link to a loan, the transfers to pair) is the writer's alone.
    2. **Empty**: a short line where the content would be (*No saved plans*), beside the
       action that makes the first one when there is one.
    3. **A count or a status**: a pill on the card's header or the row (*3 uncategorised*,
       *Code out until 5:40 PM*), its one sentence of explanation its tooltip, reached by the
       keyboard.
    4. **Something needing attention**: a notice of one line naming it, with its actions as
       buttons (*Statement overdue · Import · Type today's balance*). A warning of what an
       action will do (*No backup is made first.*) is such a notice, beside the action.
    5. **Opening a record** from a list is a tool at the end of its row, an icon-only button,
       never a click on the row. Its name says what it does and to what (*Edit Whole Foods*,
       *View Whole Foods*), as its tooltip:
       1. A **pencil** when the dialog it opens has anything the person may change. Where
          that is only their own part of a record they read (a transaction they read,
          counted in a budget of their own), the dialog holds just that part, under the
          record named once and one line of what it holds beyond its row (S12.18.6.5), and
          the pencil says so (*Leave Rent out of your budgets*).
       2. An **eye** when it is only to be read, and only where the record holds more than its
          row; a row that shows the whole of what one may only read has no tool.
       3. A row's own control stays only for the one field the row is mostly there to set (a
          transaction's category, a plan's amounts); anything else is changed in the dialog.
       4. A row that shows more of itself (a holding's lots) does so from a button on its
          name. A card or a chip that is one button (a receipt, a gift), and a link to a page,
          open as they are.
    6. **One component each** draws them, the same on every screen. A test fails on an
       action left out because of a permission on its record, on one shown unavailable
       without a reason, and on a form switched off whole.
    7. **What this doesn't cover**: the answer to an action just taken (a save's question or
       refusal, beside the buttons that took it), a tour's steps (S12.17), the screens outside
       the frame (sign-in, the welcome, the locked screen), which are one task each, results
       a screen works out (a plan's payoff, the money to move), and a security prompt that
       carries a value to compare (a certificate's fingerprint), which keeps it with it.
