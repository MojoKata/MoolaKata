# S7. Budgets, gifts and giving

1. **Budgets are per user, or per group** (a household budget a group shares at `write`).
   A budget covers chosen accounts and is monthly, with yearly lines where a category is
   planned by the year.
   1. **Shared like an account** (S10.3): its owner chooses its accounts, shares it, and
      deletes it; a `write` share also changes its plan, its moves and its leave-outs. Its
      actuals count every transaction in its accounts. Its receivers see the figures from the
      owner's own accounts, and from an account shared with the owner only if that account is
      shared with them too, so a budget never passes on an account someone else shared (R3.4).
   2. **The plan holds from a month on.** A change applies from the month it is made in,
      so past months keep the plan they had; a category taken out of the plan stops from
      that month. A plan line on a parent category counts its children with it. **Edit plan**
      and **Estimate from history** show the plan as it holds that month, each line as stored,
      never the month's figures (which add what carried over and the month's moves), so saved
      unchanged they change nothing; a line is taken out by its ✕, from that month (S12.15).
   3. **A method**, chosen when the budget is made: **tracking**, a plan per category
      compared with what is spent, received and saved (S7.3), or **envelope**, where the money
      in the budget's accounts is handed out to categories until none is left to hand out
      (S7.13). A budget keeps its method; **Copy as scenario** (S7.12.4) makes one with the
      other.
   4. **Options**, chosen when the budget is made and turned on or off later in its settings,
      so a budget shows only what it uses: **reserves** (S7.10) and **moves** (S7.11). An
      envelope budget always has moves.
   5. **Estimate from history**: making a budget, or later from its plan, offers a plan line
      per category from its average over the last 3, 6 or 12 months, as the user picks:
      monthly where it was spent in most months, yearly otherwise. The recurring transactions
      of S7.5, looked for three years back so a yearly bill has the three payments a series
      needs, are offered as lines of their own (*Rent $1,850 a month*, *Car insurance $1,140
      a year*), a yearly one as a reserve when reserves are on. Each proposed line is kept,
      changed or dropped before the plan is saved.
   6. **Where money comes from**, worked out from the budget's accounts, with nothing to set
      while their history is clear: each card's **paid from** account, learned from its paired
      payments (S1.7) and changed only where the history is mixed; for each account reserves
      are held in, its **moved from** account (S7.10.4), proposed from the account that most
      often transfers into it and changeable; and, until there is history, the budget's
      account receiving the most income. A household with several checking accounts and a
      card for each spouse needs no account named as the main one.
   7. **Its accounts over months** (R3.10): each account counts from a month (or since the
      budget began) to a month (or still). Adding an account, or taking one out, in the
      budget's settings counts from a month chosen, this month unless another is: months
      before it keep the accounts they had. One taken out from the month it began counts in
      none. A closed account counts up to the month it closed (S1.2); a deleted one, a
      correction, in none.
2. **Categories** form a two-level tree (*Housing › Rent*). Each is an **expense**,
   **income**, **savings** or **transfer** category. A default set ships, and each category
   may carry a **tax tag** (charitable, medical, business, childcare, education, …) that
   feeds Taxes (S8.5), and **Keep receipts for** a number of years, empty for ever
   (S14.10.1).
   1. **Savings** is money put away rather than spent: a transfer into an account the budget
      doesn't count (S7.3.2), or a loan's principal (S1.9.7). A plan line in a savings
      category can name an active projection (S6.7) and shows its monthly contribution beside
      the plan (*Retirement: $500 planned, your plan assumes $650*).
   2. **Shipped with the rest**: *Cash* (expense: cash taken out and spent, S7.3.2),
      *Savings* (savings: *Emergency fund*, *Retirement*, *Investing*, *Education*), and
      *Loan payments* (*Interest*, *Escrow*, *PMI and fees* as expenses, *Principal* as
      savings, S1.9.7).
3. **Each period** has, per category, the **planned** amount, the **actual**, and what
   remains.
   1. **What counts**: the transactions in the budget's accounts, by their categories:
      spending as spent, income as received, savings as saved. A split transaction counts
      through its splits. A refund in an expense category lowers its spending, in the month
      it lands. A pending transaction doesn't count until it posts. Matched transfers between
      two of the budget's accounts, and transfer categories, never count (S1.7).
   2. **Money leaving or entering the budget**: a matched transfer between one of the
      budget's accounts and an account it doesn't count counts like any transaction, by the
      category of its side in the budget: out to a brokerage account as *Savings ›
      Investing*, in from a savings account as income. One to or from a `cash` account counts
      as *Cash* unless given another category, so cash spent counts whether or not the cash
      account is in the budget. One with no category, or a transfer category, is listed under
      **Left the budget** or **Entered the budget** to sort, and counts nowhere until it is.
      Whether the other side is one of the budget's accounts is judged by the accounts the
      budget counted that month (S7.1.7), whether or not the viewer can see that account: one
      they can't is named *an account not shared*, a payment to a loan they can't see counts
      whole in its category, and one whose account was deleted since still left the budget
      (*a deleted account*).
      Making or editing a budget names the accounts it leaves out that often receive transfers
      from its accounts (*Cash gets transfers from UMB Checking: include it?*).
   3. **Loan payments** count at the bank account they come from, as their loan says
      (S1.9.7): split by the schedule into the categories of its parts, or whole in one
      category. A loan account is never one of a budget's accounts.
   4. **Reimbursable** spending (S2.9) counts only the part neither awaited nor paid back;
      a payback paired with it doesn't count as income.
   5. **Left out**: a transaction left out of a budget (a car bought outright) counts nowhere
      in it. The switch is the budget's own, in the transaction's *In budgets* line
      (S12.2.3), and Activity records it.
   6. **Gifts and Giving** take their plans from the gift and giving plans, never typed
      twice: *Gifts* is a yearly line of the budgets in the owner's gift plans for the year
      (S7.7.1), and *Giving* a yearly line of the owner's giving plan's target (S7.9.1), each
      with a link to its plan.
   7. **Rollover** carries a monthly line's remainder into the next month when the line says
      so: what is unspent adds to it, what is overspent comes off it. Without rollover the
      remainder is forgotten at the month's end. A yearly line compares with the year to
      date and does not roll over.
   8. **Overspending** is covered within the month by a move (S7.11), or left at the month's
      end, where rollover carries it negative and no rollover forgets it; an envelope budget
      can take it from the next month's *To Budget* instead (S7.13.3).
   9. **Not in the plan**: spending in a category with no plan line is listed as such.
   10. **Kept once worked out**: a month's figures are kept, and worked out again when
       anything they come from changes (a transaction, a plan line, a move, a leave-out, in
       that month or one before it). **Refresh** on a month works them out again at once.
4. **Screens** (S12.2.6): the **Budgets** list, the active budget first (S7.12), then the
   scenarios, each with its method, accounts and owner. For one budget: the month's
   categories as progress bars (green, then amber at 90%, red over; a reserve as its
   balance against its target, S7.10), income against spending and saving, cash flow (a
   Sankey from income to categories), trends of a category over 12 months, what is
   uncategorised and what left or entered the budget, to sort, **Awaiting reimbursement**
   (S2.9), the month's moves, **Where the money is** (S7.10.4), the month's review (S7.14),
   and **Compare** (S7.12.3). Reports and charts are on these screens; budgets have no
   export.
5. **Recurring** transactions (rent, subscriptions, salary) are detected from history and
   listed with their next expected date and amount. A missed or changed one is flagged.
   1. **A series** is three or more transactions in one account with the same payee (its
      reference numbers aside) and sign, at a weekly, two-weekly, twice-monthly, monthly,
      quarterly or yearly interval, give or take a few days, with amounts within half of
      the usual one. They are found in the last 15 months each time the list is shown.
   2. **Missed**: the next one is more than a few days late. **Changed**: the last amount
      moved 5% or more from the usual, flagged only where the amounts are otherwise fixed
      (a subscription, rent), since a utility bill varies anyway.
6. **Budget alerts** come from a user's active budget only (S7.12), raised as alerts to its
   owner (S12.11):
   1. **Cash before payday**, for each checking account in the budget with recurring payments
      out (S7.5): when its balance, less the recurring payments expected from it before its
      next expected money in (income landing there, or a regular transfer in), would fall
      below zero, an alert names the account and the day (*Bob's Checking dips below $0 on the
      26th, before the transfer from Joint on the 1st*). It resolves when the figures no
      longer say so.
   2. *(later)*: a category passing a threshold, a large transaction.
7. **Gift plans and occasions.** People are the household's (S10.10). On the Gifts screen
   each user keeps a **gift plan** for each person they give to or receive from:
   1. **A gift plan has** its person, the **holidays** they get gifts for, **anniversaries**
      other than a wedding (each with its own date), a yearly **gift budget** or one per
      occasion, a reminder lead time (14 days by default), and notes (sizes, likes, what
      they already have). The person's **birthday**, their **wedding anniversary** (the start
      of a *spouse* relationship) and their relation to the user (*your granddaughter*,
      S10.10.5) come from the person. Adding a gift plan for someone new adds the person
      first, as S10.10.3 says. Changing an occasion keeps the gifts planned for it; taking
      away an anniversary or other occasion that has gifts planned asks first, and they
      become ideas, with no occasion.
   2. **Holidays** ship as a list with their dates computed per year: fixed dates
      (Christmas, Valentine's Day), rule-based ones (Mother's Day, Father's Day,
      Thanksgiving), and lunar or lunisolar ones (Hanukkah, Diwali, Lunar New Year, Eid
      al-Fitr) from shipped tables, plus **custom occasions** with a date of their own.
   3. **Upcoming occasions**: the next 60 days of the dates in the user's gift plans, each with its budget,
      the gifts planned so far, and what is still missing. Those inside their lead time show
      on the Dashboard (*Emma's birthday in 9 days, no gift yet*), and an occasion entering
      its lead time raises an alert (S12.11).
8. **Gifts.**
   1. **A gift** is for one or more people and an occasion (and its year): a description, a
      **status** (*idea*, *planned*, *bought*, *given*), its cost, where it was bought, a link,
      notes, and receipts (S9). A cost is linked to its transaction, which needs write on its
      account and is recorded in Activity, or typed. One purchase can be split across several
      gifts.
   2. **Ideas** are gifts without a date: a running list per person, to pick from when an
      occasion comes.
   3. **Gifts received** can be recorded too (from whom, for what), with a **thank-you
      sent** tick.
   4. **History**: per person, what was given in past years (so it isn't given twice) and
      the spending per year against their budget. Per year: gift spending by person and by
      occasion. Gift transactions are categorised *Gifts* in budgets (S7.2).
   5. **Privacy**: gift plans and gifts are their owner's alone. A user may share a gift
      plan (with its gifts) with another user or group, read or write, so a couple plans
      their children's gifts together. A gift for a person who is a user is **never shown
      to that user** until it is received, even through a share, nor anything kept with it
      (a receipt, S9.6), so a surprise stays one. For a
      user of a linked server, the owner's server leaves those gifts out before sending, so
      they never reach the other server.
   6. **A deleted person**: gifts bought, given or received stay, still count in past
      spending, and show the person as *Emma (deleted)* (R3.10). Ideas and planned gifts
      for that person alone are deleted with them; a shared idea loses only that person.
   7. **Gift-tax flag**: money given to one person in a year (cash gifts, and 529
      contributions to any of the user's 529s, whether or not the beneficiary has a gift
      plan, a five-year election counting a fifth in each of its years, S1.8.2) above the
      annual exclusion is flagged for Form 709 (S8.11).
9. **Charitable giving.**
   1. **A giving plan** per year, per user or per group (as budgets are): a **target** as a
      **percentage of income** (10% by default when one is set), or a fixed amount. It counts
      its owner's accounts only: sharing it shows them, and never reads the accounts of those
      it is shared with.
   2. **Income**, for the percentage, is one of: the year's transactions in *Income*
      categories (take-home pay, the default), a typed **gross income** (from a W-2 or a pay
      stub), or a typed estimate of AGI. The plan says which it uses.
   3. **Donations** come from transactions categorised under *Giving* (tax tag *charitable*,
      S7.2), or are recorded directly: the organisation, date, amount, and **type**: cash
      (card, cheque, transfer), **goods** (with their fair market value), **appreciated
      securities** (shares from a lot, S2.2: no gain is realized, and the deduction is the
      market value when held over a year, otherwise the lesser of the market value and the
      basis), a **qualified charitable distribution** from an IRA (from age 70½, up to the
      year's limit in the tax tables; checking the age, the limit and that it came from an IRA
      comes later, [later work](later.md), L7), a **contribution to a donor-advised fund**, or
      a **grant** from one.
   4. **What counts toward the target**: everything that left the user's hands, with a DAF
      counted when it is funded (the default) or when it grants, as the plan chooses. A DAF
      contribution is never counted twice.
   5. **Organisations**: the name, EIN (optional), whether gifts to it are **deductible**
      (a 501(c)(3) charity, or not, such as a political campaign or a person), causes (tags),
      and **pledges** (an amount a month or a year, shown as expected donations). A pledge
      is **due** from the 25th of the month for a monthly one, and from December 1 for a
      yearly one, while less than it has been given to the organisation in that month or year;
      a due pledge raises an alert to its owner (S12.11.1.3), resolved by the donation.
   6. **The Giving screen**: given against the target and against the pace to date, the
      projected year end, giving by organisation, cause and type, the DAF's balance and its
      grants, the percentage of income over past years, and the donations missing an
      acknowledgement that the tax rules require (S8.9).
10. **Reserves** (a budget's option, S7.1.4): money set aside in a category month by month
    toward a **target**, for a cost that comes now and then (car repairs, a roof, a
    holiday).
    1. **A reserve** is a monthly plan line with a target. Each month it puts in its planned
       amount, but never more than reaches the target, and spending in its category takes
       from it. Its **balance** is worked out, never stored: last month's balance, plus what
       was put in and moved in, less what was spent and moved out. At the target it is
       **Funded** and puts in nothing; once spending draws it below, it puts in again. A
       target changes from a month on, as the plan does.
    2. **Shown** as its balance against its target (*$3,200 of $5,000, 64%*), with spending
       as a withdrawal (*−$700 Midas*), never as overspending while the balance covers it.
       Spending beyond the balance leaves it negative, carried until money is put back or
       moved in.
    3. **Kept**: a reserve always rolls over. Taking its category out of the plan, or its
       target off, asks first and names the balance that stops being kept; a balance is never
       carried into a line without a target.
    4. **Held in**: a reserve can name the account its money is kept in (*Ally Savings*), one
       of the budget's accounts, and different reserves can be held in different accounts (an
       emergency fund and the roof at Ally, the rest elsewhere). **Where the money is** shows,
       for each account reserves are held in, each reserve held there with its balance, their
       total, the account's balance, its withdrawals this month against **Withdrawals allowed
       a month** (S1.2), and **to move**: what the reserves held there have gained since each
       began (put in and moved in, less spent and moved out), less what was transferred into
       the account from any of the budget's other accounts over that time, net of what was
       transferred out, and less the landed money given to them (S7.10.5). Which checking
       account a transfer came from doesn't matter. A transfer in that is savings of the
       account's own, not the reserves', is listed there too and can be **kept as the account's
       own**, after which it doesn't count as moved in. When the reserves are ahead, the
       difference is to move from the account's **moved from** account (S7.1.6; *$345 to move
       from UMB Checking to Ally Savings*); when they are behind, after a repair paid from
       checking or a card, it goes back to it, summed into one transfer for the month (*$700
       to move from Ally Savings to UMB Checking*), with a warning when that transfer would go
       past the withdrawals allowed (*This would be the 7th withdrawal from Ally this month;
       it allows 6*); when they agree, the account is **backed**. An account whose balance is
       below its reserves' total shows the **shortfall**. A difference belongs to the account,
       never to one reserve, since one transfer covers several.
       MoolaKata moves no money: the transfer made at the bank arrives with an import and
       pairs by S1.7, and the account reads backed. A savings account with sub-accounts can
       give each reserve its own, exact. A reserve held nowhere reads *in the budget's
       accounts*. Only the active budget says what to move (S7.12.2); a scenario shows its
       own figures.
    5. **Landed money**: money that arrives in an account reserves are held in other than by
       a transfer from the budget's accounts (its interest, a paycheck split into it, a
       transfer from an account the budget doesn't count) is listed under **Where the money
       is** as **unassigned** until the user gives it, with a line for each reserve held
       there and one for the account's own (*not set aside*): the lines start spread among
       the reserves by balance, any may change, and what no line takes stays unassigned. Each
       line given something is a move (S7.11), saved together; money given to a reserve
       counts as moved in, so *to move* never asks for it again. In the budget it counts as it always does: interest as income, a
       transfer by S7.3.2.
11. **Moves** (a budget's option, always on in an envelope budget): a **move** puts an
    amount from one category to another, a reserve among them, for one month (*$40 from Entertainment to Dining,
    March*), and each one's available amount that month changes by it. Where the line rolls
    over, or is a reserve, the move carries on with its remainder. In an envelope budget a
    move can also come from or go to *To Budget* (*$50 more to Gifts this December*), or go
    into a card's payment line (S7.13.2).
    1. **Made by hand**, never on its own: an overspent row offers **Cover from…**, listing
       the categories and reserves with money left that month, the most first, so an
       emergency fund covers a hospital bill in *Health*; any row offers **Move**.
       A move has its amount, its month and an optional note.
    2. **Listed** in its month (**Moves**) and on both rows (*+$40 from Entertainment*,
       *−$40 to Dining*), and recorded in Activity. Deleting a move undoes it. A move in a
       past month changes that month and what carries after it, as any edit of history does
       (S2.8), and flags the month if it was reviewed (S7.14).
    3. **The budget's own**: two budgets over the same accounts keep their own moves. The
       owner and a `write` share make them; a `read` share sees them.
12. **The active budget and scenarios.** Each user follows at most one **active** budget,
    among those they own or that are shared with them. The others are **scenarios**, to try
    another method or plan beside it.
    1. **Make active** asks before turning off the one it replaces. It rewrites nothing:
       each budget keeps its months and its decisions, and Activity records the change. A
       user's first budget is made active.
    2. **Only the active budget** feeds the Dashboard's card (S12.2.1), raises budget alerts
       (S7.6) and says what to move (S7.10.4). A scenario shows a banner (*Scenario: not the
       budget you follow*) with **Make active** and **Compare**.
    3. **Compare** shows the active budget and one scenario side by side for a month or a
       range of months: per category, what is available, spent and remaining, the reserves'
       balances, and *To Budget* for an envelope budget, with the totals and where the two
       part.
    4. **Copy as scenario…** makes a budget with the same accounts and plan, in the method
       chosen (a tracking line becomes the same amount assigned each month, and an envelope
       line the same amount planned), with this month's moves when asked.
    5. **Each user's own choice**: a household budget shared at `write` can be active for
       both, while one of them tries a scenario of their own; a scenario is shared as any
       budget is. A linked household's budgets are seen in its window, never made active
       here.
    6. **The same in every budget**: a transaction's category, splits, tags and reimbursable
       mark are the transaction's, and changing one says it changes every budget that counts
       it. A budget's own decisions are its plan, its moves and its leave-outs.
13. **Envelope budgets.** The money in the budget's accounts is handed out to categories
    each month until none is left to hand out.
    1. **To Budget** is the cash in the budget's accounts, less what its categories hold and
       what is set aside for its cards (S7.13.2). It starts in the budget's first month, from
       those accounts' balances that day, and there is none in the months before. Each month the
       plan's lines assign their amounts (a yearly line a twelfth of its amount), a move from or
       to *To Budget* assigns more or less, and income adds to it as it lands.
       Below zero it shows red: more is assigned than there is.
    2. **Cards**: each card among the budget's accounts has a **payment** line, worked out,
       never stored. A charge to the card from a category with money in it moves that much
       from the category to the payment line, and a payment to the card takes from it. A
       part payment leaves the rest on the line, its cash still in the account that pays
       the card (S7.1.6) and not offered again by *To Budget*. A charge beyond what its
       category held leaves the category at nothing rather than below it, so the debt never
       adds to *To Budget*; that part, and a balance owed when the budget began, are **owed,
       not backed**, shown on the card's line until a move into the line covers them (from a
       category, *To Budget*, or a *Card payoff* reserve whose target is the amount owed).
       Interest and fees are spending in their category.
    3. **A category's balance** always carries into the next month. One overspent at the
       month's end is carried negative (the default), or taken from the next month's *To
       Budget* when its line says so.
    4. **Money leaving** the budget's accounts (S7.3.2) leaves *To Budget*'s cash with it.
14. **The month's review**, offered from the 1st for the month before: what was overspent,
    what is to move (S7.10.4), what is left in *To Budget*, and what is uncategorised or left
    or entered the budget, to sort, each with its action. **Mark reviewed** records who and
    when. A reviewed month whose figures later change (a late transaction, a recategorised
    one, a move in it) is flagged *changed since reviewed*, with what changed, until it is
    reviewed again. Months are never locked, since history can be edited (S2.8).
